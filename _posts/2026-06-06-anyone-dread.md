> What if some day or night a demon were to steal after you into your loneliest loneliness, and say to you, “This life as you now live it and have lived it, will be completed erased, and there will be no evidence of your existence, every pain and every joy and every thought and sigh and everything unutterably small or great in your life will be erased, all gone with the wind, and replaced by a random identity, a random life, a random family, and random memories” Would you not throw yourself down and gnash your teeth and curse the demon who thus spoke? Or would a sudden, icy dread wash over you as the demon smiles and whispers: "Why do you tremble? Are you so certain we have not already made this deal, and the life you now live is simply the one you drew blindly from the dark?"

<small> The presentation was inspired after Nietzsche’s Eternal Recurrence.</small>

How is choosing a random life any different from being born? I call this thought experiment “Anyone dread”, it’s a simple fact that the conscious you (ego) could have been anyone, like someone who’s in great joy at having hiked a huge mountain, or someone who’s at complete peace after receiving a hug from their loved one, or someone who’s in deep pain after unluckily getting his legs broken and being crippled for life, or someone who died at the very first bullets from the enemy on the other side, who promised to his wife and children that he would come back home. It’s strange because they experienced it just the same way you would have experienced it if it were you. And the strangest fact is, for them, it is “you”.

When you strip away the boundaries of the self, the sheer amount of suffering in this world, suddenly feels intensely personal. But the same doesn't hold for joy in this world, does it? I called it Anyone Dread for a reason, because you feel more dread rather than joy at the prospect of a random life. So does that mean we’re just more prone to feel dread more than joy or that there is more suffering in the world? The former makes sense to me through evolutionary reasonings, but I don’t have an answer for the latter. This awful dreadful feeling is driven by empathy, in this particular case — Universal empathy. It scares you because you recognize the reality of other people's suffering, and realize that fundamentally, the consciousness experiencing that pain is the same as yours.

Now entertain this thought experiment in the style of Rawls’, imagine if there was just the God, and you. The God comes to you as says, “I’m going to lend you a bit of my power, the power to create a human society in the planet called Earth, however you wish. But after you create the society, you will be transformed into a normal random human being who will be part of the same society as everyone else. How would you build the society?” Now wouldn’t you build a society which has the least probability of anyone suffering unnecessarily. It is because you could be anyone, it is in our best interest to minimize the unnecessarily suffering for everyone (unnecessary suffering because the growth, meaning behind necessary suffering is whole another topic). Although in reality, you have limited power and time, but the logic of changing the world for better still applies. You want the world to be better for the future generation than what you were served. I imagine every parent experiencing a feeling akin to this when their child is born. But there is another solution to this immense suffering; wipe out all consciousness, so there’s no suffering. But consciousness is rarer than black holes, and there might be something valuable about it to just throw away without care.

The truth right now is, you were assigned this life, or rather born into it. Born to a certain pair of parents, in a specific body and environment. That can’t be changed, but you are still an agent in this world. You still have control over how your life, and how you can change and adapt in the sands of time (ignoring the physics argument of free will). The randomness is precisely what makes this life irreplaceable. You didn't earn it, you didn't choose it, but this is the one you got. The fact that your “I” can’t be changed anymore, that this is all you’ll get to experience the reality as, makes you accept both the good and bad, your past, your present, and open handedly welcome the future. Essentially the crux of _Amor fati_. This makes your life all the more important (especially to you).
