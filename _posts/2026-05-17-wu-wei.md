Wu Wei is a foundational concept in the Daoist philosophy, and translates to “effortless action”, “actionless action” or you can say it’s the Art of doing nothing. Well that doesn’t mean you should be lazy and slack off, but instead it’s about aligning your life in a way that things feel effortless. It can also be called the flow state.

In any areas of life in which we want to get efficient or good at, we should let the unconscious mind handle it. Be it driving, sports, writing, dancing, even math for that matter. Take for example when you are learning to ride a motorcycle, you need to be aware of the speed, the gear shifting, clutch, breaking, and all the while managing balance. But after you learn, you can easily navigate traffic while ranting about the GTA 6 release date on a phone call. This reminds me of a proverb in Kannada, "ಕಲಿಯೋ ತನಕ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮ ವಿದ್ಯೆ, ಕಲಿತ ಮೇಲೆ ಕೋತಿ ವಿದ್ಯೆ" (Kaliyo tanaka brahma vidye, kalita mele kapi vidye). Translates to ("Until you learn it, it's divine knowledge; once you learn it, it's monkey business.") That’s the effortless action, you’re not demanding your conscious mind to do a certain task, it just happens automatically.

> **Note:** I'm using 'unconscious' loosely to mean the automatic, non-deliberate processing.

When this is applied on more than just activities or tasks, but rather on career, entrepreneurship, profession or any long term endeavor, I’d like to call it, _Eventual Wu Wei_. Naval said, “Do what feels like play to you, but like work to others”. But it would be a mistake to assume you’ll immediately feel it like play. Some are driven by extrinsic motivation, or a need to prove something, or have no other choice. But if you keep practicing it, and if it energizes you instead of draining, and you start getting good at it, it is play and you’ve achieved eventual wu wei. Well it’s not the case that you’ll be in continuous flow state thereafter. The point is you won’t feel like you’re fighting your mind to solve a problem, you’ll be fighting the problem. You could argue, “If it feels like playing, what if I get bored of it? What then?”. Maybe boredom is the signal that you've mastered the current level and the unconscious needs a harder problem to chew on. Or maybe the play was always extrinsic and you fooled yourself.

So how do we get our unconscious mind to take over the wheel? Practice is the only key to delegate stuff to the unconscious. You can’t consciously force yourself to feel joy in something or be good at it, but only the conscious mind can do the training. Wu Wei can be achieved by having both of the conscious and the unconscious minds working hand in hand towards a goal. The conscious mind trains the unconscious mind to change and update (essentially hard-wiring in the brain), while the unconscious mind plays the game efficiently. That is practice. That is learning. That is the work.

It’s true that you can apply Wu Wei on the path to stupid addictions which might be easier than you think. When you’ve trained your unconscious mind on the wrong thing (by your own definition), you have essentially developed a bad habit. Like reaching out to your phone to open Twitter seconds after closing it, even when you know well enough you should be working for the meeting tomorrow. But as with all learning (or unlearning if you will) requires time and effort. So initially, retraining will feel like a chore, even painful sometimes, but that’s the point. Of course, it’ll be hard.

I think that many of the meaning-related problems humans face is because our conscious mind is not aligned with our unconscious mind that evolution shaped, or at least we haven’t spent enough time on it to adapt. Joscha Bach’s view of consciousness is that it was born out of the need to solve hard problems in survival. So in a way consciousness is not suffering, it’s the solution to suffering. Consciousness made us aware of our own shortcomings and weakness, so that we can work towards improvement through the use of tools and creations. Consciousness is evolution's gift for improving ourselves on a timescale evolution itself can't match. But of course too much of self-consciousness is not good either, it’ll turn you into an overwhelmed philosopher.
